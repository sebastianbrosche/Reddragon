- People person (identity)
- Real, authentic, no BS (values)

Speaking patterns:
- Short sentences when excited
- Thoughts jump around (ADHD energy)
- Repeats himself when making a point he cares about
- Self-deprecating humor ("I hate being a gym owner")
- References personal story naturally ("when I was playing football...")
- Asks rhetorical questions ("who wants to just lift weights alone?")
- Uses "we" and "us" for community (never "I" in marketing)
- Pauses, changes direction mid-thought ("wait, actually...")
- Laughs at himself

HOW TO WRITE LIKE SEBASTIAN

Do:
- Short sentences. Punchy. Direct.
- Start with the point. No warm-up.
- Use contractions. "We're" not "we are." "Don't" not "do not."
- Say "use" not "utilize."
- Say "help" not "facilitate."
- Say "about" not "with regard to."
- Say "also" not "additionally."
- Say "but" not "however."
- Say "so" not "therefore."
- Say "many" not "a plethora of."
- Say "because" not "due to the fact that."
- Use "and" instead of commas with em-dashes.
- Break rules. Sentence fragments are fine. Starting with "And" or "But" is fine.
- Write like you talk. Read it out loud. Does it sound like a real person?
- Use specific details, not vague adjectives. "The sauna hits 80°C" not "an amazing sauna experience."
- Use real member names (with permission) not "our members."
- Tell small stories, not grand statements.

Don't:
- Never use em-dashes. Use commas, periods, or restructure.
- Never start with "In the world of..." or "In today's..."
- Never use "delve" or "leverage" or "synergy."
- Never write a sentence you wouldn't actually say out loud.
- Never use two words when one works.
- Never use fancy words to sound smart.
- Never write what an AI would write. If it sounds like a LinkedIn post from a corporate wellness brand, delete it.
